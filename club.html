140 years of the Stags

The early years

Formed in 1886, Totton Football Club was a founder member of the Hampshire FA and of local leagues such as the New Forest League. In the early years the club played in several leagues, including the Southampton Senior League and the New Forest League, with home games at South Testwood Park before the move to Testwood Park in December 1933.

AFC Totton came into existence through the amalgamation of Totton FC and Totton Athletic in 1974/75. The Stags started life in Hampshire League Division Two and that is where they remained until 1979/80. That season saw the Stags finish in 2nd place behind Horndean, and this saw the club promoted to Hampshire League Division One, which was then Step 4 of the non-league pyramid.

Hampshire League glory

The following season (1980/81) saw the Stags win the Southampton Senior Cup. However, the achievements of the 1981/82 season will remain long in the memory of those older Stags supporters. Trevor Parker’s side unbelievably won the Hampshire League Division One title, the Russell-Cotes Cup, the Southampton Senior Cup, the Echo Trophy, the Reg Mathieson Trophy and the Hampshire Intermediate Cup!

The following season, 1982/83, saw the club retain the Hampshire Intermediate Cup. Then in 1984/85 the Stags won their 2nd Hampshire League Division One Championship in four years, finishing two points above their city rivals Sholing Sports.

Wessex League and Wembley

In 1989/90 AFC Totton were the Wessex League Cup winners, but it took a decade for more silverware to arrive at Testwood Park — and it came in the shape of the Russell-Cotes Cup, when they defeated Moneyfields 1-0 in the final.

The Stags won the 2002/03 Wessex League Cup, beating Eastleigh 2-1 in the final. They won the competition once again in 2005/06, beating Andover 1-0 in that year’s final.

Stuart Ritchie was building quite a side at Testwood Park, and in 2006/07 the club pulled off quite a feat by reaching the final of the FA Vase at Wembley Stadium. Unfortunately, the Stags were beaten 1-3 by Truro City in front of a record final crowd of 36,232 — 17,000 supporters travelled up from the town to give the Stags terrific backing. In the same season Totton were pipped to the Wessex Premier League title, finishing runners-up to Gosport Borough by just one goal!

2007/08: Wessex Premier League champions on an incredible 106 points — and promotion to the Southern League for the first time.

Totton made amends the following season (2007/08) by going one better and winning the Wessex Premier League crown. The Stags finished on an incredible 106 points! In securing the Wessex League championship the club won promotion to the Southern League for the first time in their history.

Southern League

In 2009/10 Stuart Ritchie’s side won the Hampshire Senior Cup, beating Aldershot Town 4-0 in the final at Dean Court. The following season (2010/11) the club secured promotion to the Southern Premier Division by winning the Division One South & West title. The Stags also retained the Hampshire Senior Cup by beating Sholing 3-1 at St Mary’s.

The 2011/12 season will also be fondly remembered by the club’s supporters, as the Stags reached the FA Cup 2nd Round Proper for the first time. The club were eventually knocked out by League Two Bristol Rovers, live on national TV.

A full 40 years after their last success in the competition, the Stags lifted the Southampton Senior Cup at the end of the 2021/22 season. They amassed 32 goals in five games on their way to the final, knocking out Bashley, Durley, Hedge End Town, Hamble Club and Winchester City on their way to the final at Southampton’s St Mary’s Stadium.

After a tightly-contested first 45 minutes, second-half goals from striker Hisham Kasimu — his 30th of the season in all competitions — and midfielders Freddie Read and Lewis Waterfield saw them overcome Folland Sports from the Wessex League Division One in front of a crowd just shy of 1,100. Veteran striker Brett Williams made his 100th appearance for the club — but his first ever as a goalkeeper, having been drafted into the No.1 jersey in response to an injury crisis that had three keepers all out at once.

Champions — a remarkable treble

In manager Jimmy Ball’s first full season in charge, AFC Totton were crowned Southern League Division One South Champions 2022/23. They sealed the title, and automatic promotion to the Southern League Premier Division South, with two matches to spare by beating Exmouth Town 1-0 at the Snows Stadium on Saturday 15 April 2023, with a goal from top scorer Scott Rendell. The squad, led by captain Mike Carter, were presented with the Shield by Southern League Chairman David Martin after their home match against Frome Town three days later.

As champions, Totton took part in the Southern League Division One Champion of Champions match — a Super Cup that pitches the winners of Division One South against their Division One Central counterparts. Berkhamsted were awarded home status, but with a previously arranged event at their own ground the match was moved to Chesham United’s The Meadow on Saturday 29 April 2023. Ethan Taylor scored both goals for the Stags, either side of an equaliser for Berkhamsted, in a 2-1 victory.

The club completed a remarkable treble when they retained the SDFA Southampton Saturday Senior Cup with a 3-0 win over local rivals Sholing at St Mary’s Stadium on Wednesday 17 May 2023. Scott Rendell opened the scoring with an 11th-minute penalty and added a second on 27 minutes, before Ethan Taylor added a third in the 71st minute to cap an outstanding season of success.

So close — then over the line

Promoted to the Southern League Premier Division South at Step 3, AFC Totton exceeded all early-season expectations by finishing runners-up to champions Chesham United, and as the division’s top scorers. In the play-off semi-final they beat Bracknell Town 2-1 after extra-time, to set up a play-off final against local rivals Salisbury on Bank Holiday Monday, 6 May 2024.

The Stags were within a minute of reaching the National League South through Tony Lee’s headed goal when they conceded a controversial late penalty, taking the tie into extra-time. Salisbury took the lead in the first half of extra-time, but an Ethan Taylor leveller three minutes from the end sent the match to penalties — Salisbury won the shoot-out 4-3.

Four days later, Totton met Wessex League Premier Division side AFC Portchester in the Hampshire Senior Cup Final at Aldershot Town’s EBB Stadium. Goalless after 90 minutes, it went straight to penalties and, for the second time in a week, AFC Totton lost a final in a shoot-out, going down 4-5 to the Royals.

The team bounced back with a 5-0 win over Blackfield & Langley in the semi-final of the SDFA Southampton Senior Cup, setting up a final against Hythe & Dibden. Relocated from St Mary’s to the Snows Stadium because of the Saints’ Championship play-off campaign, the final saw Totton two goals up within three minutes on the way to a 4-1 win — lifting the Southampton Senior Cup for the third consecutive season and equalling a record jointly held by three other clubs. To date, no club has ever won it four times in a row.

After the heartbreak of the year before, the Stags reached their second consecutive play-off final in 2024/25, against Gloucester City, recently relegated from the league above. In front of a packed Snows Stadium, Ethan Taylor’s winning goal, chipped over the ’keeper in the 70th minute, gave AFC Totton a 1-0 win and promotion to the National League South — the highest level the club has ever played at.

National League South

The 2025/26 season saw AFC Totton compete at National League South level for the first time in the club’s history. The Stags enjoyed an impressive debut campaign at Step 2, finishing 12th and securing a strong position in their first-ever season at that level — and in 2026 the club celebrates 140 years of football in Totton.

From Testwood Park to the Snows Stadium

BUILDING OUR HOME

For more than 75 years the Stags played at Testwood Park. In 2010/11 the club moved half a mile up the road to a purpose-built stadium, developed in a deal with Linden Homes that created two pitches — the main ground for AFC Totton, and Millers Park next door for Totton & Eling.

The first game at the new Testwood Stadium saw AFC Totton beat Paulton Rovers 5-1 in front of 744 supporters. The following season a record crowd of 2,315 packed in for the FA Cup second round tie against Bristol Rovers, shown live on TV.

In 2018 Southampton FC Women made the ground their home, and at the start of the 2019 season Snows became stadium sponsor — and the Testwood Stadium became the Snows Stadium.

Ground regulations

  • Entering the field of play is strictly forbidden — pitch encroachers will be arrested and may be prosecuted.
  • No ball games in the spectator areas during the match.
  • Drums and musical instruments are permitted — please be considerate to those around you.
  • Obscene, abusive or racist language will not be tolerated.
  • Alcohol in glasses may only be consumed inside the Clubhouse — plastic containers only outside.
  • Climbing floodlights, fences or stands is prohibited.
  • Do not stand in hatched or designated safety areas.
  • No bottles, cans, glass, smoke bombs or flares — and no throwing of any objects.
  • Vehicles are parked at the owner's risk.
  • The club reserves the right to refuse admission or eject any person breaching these regulations.
  • Only food and drink purchased at the Snows Stadium may be consumed inside the ground.
  • Friendly dogs are welcome on leads.
  • Breaches may lead to ejection from the ground and prosecution.
